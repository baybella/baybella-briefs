2The Rule It Lives Bywhy it's not a testimonial
  • The proof is the span, not a claim. Nobody says "these last months." You WATCH the same pair clip on across a visibly changing person, and the counter does the arguing.
  • Longevity is the one told-angle you can actually show. A climbing counter over a real timeline demonstrates it. That's why this angle earns a silent b-roll meme where cost or comfort could not.
  • Cost lands anchored to the ACT, never a floating number. The close ties the money to the visible thing on screen (the same pair, since January), so it reads as a fact about her life, not a price tag bolted on.
  • No talking, no hook line, no CTA. On-screen text + trending audio carry it. Product named only in a soft end-frame.
3The Wear-Counter Treatment (locked)the device that makes it land
  • A single counter pinned in a corner (e.g. top-right), same position the whole video: Wear 001 ticking up fast as we cut through the clips.
  • It climbs in step with the cuts - every new clip (new hair, new outfit, new season) jumps the number higher, so the eye reads "still the same pair, still going."
  • Cap the visible number to what the span defends. Tie the climb to the real number of clips / months we actually have of her. Land it on a round, honest end number (e.g. Wear 300 only if the archive span genuinely supports a pair worn near-daily across that stretch). Do NOT inflate it past what one pair over the real span would give - an over-the-top number torpedoes it in a scam-sensitive category.
  • Let the counter freeze on the final number as the last clip settles, then the cost line drops.
4On-Screen Text Beats (locked)these are the compliance surface
Opening frame (over the first, oldest clip): "same pair of lashes. every clip is a different month."

The counter (pinned corner, climbing all the way through): Wear 001 → … → [final honest number]

Closing frame (counter frozen, last clip settled): "same pair since January. that's my whole lash budget, sorted."

"January" is a placeholder - swap it for the real start month of this creator's oldest clip so the span is true. If her oldest clip isn't January, use whatever month it actually is.

6Assembly Notes
  • The application is real, keep it real. Every clip is genuine archive of her clipping the magnetic lash on - that's the whole moat. Never AI-generate or re-stage a lash-on-eye moment (AI can't render believable magnetic lashes on a real face).
  • Show the clip-on, not just the worn result. Favour clips where you actually see the lash going ON - the recurring "same action, same pair" across looks is what sells the longevity.
  • Pace: ~20-25s. Quick through the middle so the changing looks flip past, then let the final clip + cost line settle.
  • Framing: normal flattering close-ups, whatever the archive gives - no need to re-crop to macro.
  • Audio: a current trending sound, no VO. Pick one with a build/drop so the counter climb rides the build and the final number lands on the drop.
  • Product naming: end-frame / caption only. Never spoken.
Truth check on the counter: the final wear number must be defensible off ONE pair over the REAL archive span (kit basis is ~115 wears/pair, ~8 months of daily wear). If the span doesn't support a big number, use the honest smaller one - the timeline is impressive on its own. No inflated zeros anywhere (the kit has a real price; never imply "free").
Option - stack 2-3 creators: run the same treatment on two or three creators back-to-back (each her own mini wear-counter, each visibly aged across her own span), then a shared final frame. This layers longevity (the counter) WITH social proof (multiple different women, same pair each). If we do this, keep each creator's counter honest to HER real span, and keep the cost close once at the very end, not per creator.
7Compliance (checked)on-screen text only - no talking to gate
  • No false zeros. Cost close is "my whole lash budget, sorted" (one real $69 kit IS the budget) - never "free," "nothing," "costs me zero."
  • No scold, no put-down. Pure flex about her own pair. No enemy, no "still rebuying?" jab at anyone.
  • Flex not settle. "sorted" reads as savvy and handled, not apologetic-cheap.
  • No em-dashes anywhere in the on-screen text (checked).
  • "Reusable" avoided - the longevity lands as "same pair since [month]," never the un-premium "reusable."
  • Every claim literally true - the counter is defended by the real archive span; the month is the creator's real start month.
