What they see

1

Your hook is what they see, too

The first second is a picture before it's a sentence

Notice

  • 🔍
    How close they getFace or eye fills the screen. No wide shots of the room.
  • 👁️
    The lashes are on from second oneApplicator at the eye in the first frame, not halfway through.
  • ☀️
    Soft, bright lightFacing a window or a soft lamp. No shadows on the face.
  • 📱
    A sharp cameraBack camera if you can, clean lens, highest quality setting.
  • 🧼
    A clean backgroundNothing behind you pulling focus off your eyes.

What you say

Based on our ad performance data, our best-performing hooks generally contain these four things:

1. SimpleEasy to follow the first time you hear it. No explaining needed.
2. Attention-grabbingSomething she doesn't expect to hear: a bold opinion, a confession, or a problem she knows too well.
3. A loopTease what's coming, then hold back the why.
4. HopefulShe should feel this might finally be the one.

Quick checks: one point only · end on a statement, not a question

Your on-screen text is a different job from what you say. See the editing guide.

Write your hooks

Now write yours. Three hooks that are genuinely different, not one idea three ways. You'll film each one as its own take.

Then sharpen it

✂️Cut it downFewest words, most punch.
👁️Verbs you can seemelted, peeled, smudged
💬Your wordsNot a brand's.
🎬Paint the momentNot the benefit.
😅Sound like a personA bit imperfect, a bit funny.

Rules

  • Show it while you say it (one eye vs the other)
  • One number at most
  • One point only
  • End on a statement, not a question
  • Stressed word in CAPITALS
