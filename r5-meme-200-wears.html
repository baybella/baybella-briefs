01What We’re Makingthe concept

An up-close b-roll clip. One short, silent, close-up shot of you clipping a magnetic lash on. We lay a single line of text over it and pair it with a trending sound.

The point: the same pair, worn over and over, and it still clips on like new. That’s the whole flex.

★Agency Notesinternal - not for the creator

Format: single-clip close-up b-roll meme. One creator, one silent 5-8 second up-close application clip (one eye, clip on, show the eye). Format-key = close-up-broll; reference reel = the brand’s own up-close b-roll (video 7610249714339958036). Same DNA as winners ①/⑩ (application-clip proof), stripped to a single reach asset. NOT a compilation, so no snippet pay - this is one creator’s flat rate for one clip.

Job: top-funnel REACH, not a closer. Longevity is the angle, and it’s the right fit for b-roll because the footage plus the overlay demonstrates it (you watch the same well-worn pair still clip on cleanly). Pair it with a closer (the cost/value or fit work) in the release window; do not grade it on its own conversion.

Overlay line (POST - we add it, single line over the clip): “one pair. over 100 wears. still clipping on.” This carries the locked meaning (one pair, worn a lot, still going) while staying literally true against the product fact (~115 wears per pair). We deliberately did NOT write “200-something wears” on a single pair - that overshoots the ~115/pair basis and trips the truth gate (G11, no inflated claims) in a scam-sensitive category. “over 100 wears” is defensible and still an outsized-longevity flex. If a bigger number is wanted, anchor it to the whole KIT (two pairs, ~230 wears) and reword to “one kit”, never “one pair”.

Mechanism: the clip is silent, so “magnetic” lands in the on-screen text / caption (agency job), never a spoken line. The overlay above already implies the clip-on; add “magnetic, no glue” to the caption for the mandatory mechanism-naming.

Register: flex-not-settle. It’s a quiet brag about how long one pair lasts, not a dig at anyone still rebuying. No scold, no “you’re still…” framing, no year stamp.

Two-trays value (AI-3): consciously SKIPPED on the CLIP itself (a single-line meme can carry only one idea - longevity). It can live in the CAPTION as a light aside if wanted (“and the kit comes with a whole spare pair”), since longevity and the two-pair value are coupled - agency’s call, do not crowd the overlay.

Round 5 audience/callout (AI-4): script-target the verified 35-54 buyer (longevity/value-per-wear lands hardest with her), cast a YOUNG creator per the locked concept (the up-close eye is age-neutral on a silent clip, so young casting doesn’t fight the 35-54 message). No spoken callout; the overlay is the targeting frame. Founder’s call on any further callout.

Villain: none (locked). A no-villain longevity flex is fully valid and does not bundle into a shared-enemy audience.

Compliance: clean application only, no fumble on camera (a fumble sells the problem). No claim beyond what the clip shows plus the defensible “over 100 wears” longevity figure. Trending audio must be a commercially-cleared / licensed track for paid use, not just a viral organic sound.
