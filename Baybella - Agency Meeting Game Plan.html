Agency Meeting — Game Plan

With Josh / Ecom Republic · Goodwill-first · You're right-sizing from a position of strength

🎯 Your Objective — walk out with these

  1. June fee reduced as far as reasonable They did some (lighter-touch) work — so not a hard $5k. Push the fee down, proportionate to the lighter scope. Directionally down; get as much off as you can.
  2. Rizza waste covered — ~$2,150 The double-booked creator briefs left you owing creators for content you can't use. Get it credited / made whole.
  3. Accountability + clean go-forward Clear on who owns what now that you're taking creative in-house.

Posture

  • Warm, straight, high-status. A sharp founder making a disciplined correction — not a client pleading.
  • Own the decision (you overextended on cost). Never plead "I can't afford you" — that hands him your desperation.
  • This is not a tribunal. You're not here to nitpick the work.

The Ladder — run it in this order

1
Open — own your side (disarms him)
"I'll be straight — I took on more cost infrastructure than made sense for where the business was, heading into a tough patch. That's on me, and I'm right-sizing it. I'm not here to nitpick the team's work."
2
The ask — goodwill on June
"What I'd really value is some give on June — starting the $5k structure from this month rather than carrying the full fee through a month we're already transitioning the creative out of. It'd make a real difference right now."
3
Economic context — say once, gently, as value not blame
"Part of why the maths stopped working: the account's still largely running on creative I made over a year ago — the newer work just wasn't outperforming it. Not blaming anyone, the market's been brutal. It's just the value reality that made carrying the full cost hard to justify."
Supports "I overextended." Pre-empts "but you're getting value." Say it once, move on. Do NOT turn it into "you failed me."
4
The one concrete — Rizza (~$2,150)
"Separately — this one's specific — the creator briefs got double-booked, same single-creator brief to multiple creators, which leaves me owing ~$2,150 for content I can't use. That's a cost the coordination created, and I'd like us to square it as part of this."
Frame as damage to make whole, not a grievance.
5
Google waste · SKIP unless truly nothing to lose
(Only if he's fully stonewalling and the relationship's already over:) "An independent review found ~75% of my Google budget going to bidding on my own brand."
You've never discussed Google with him — springing it reads as a random stretch / bait-and-switch. Default: don't use it. Your strong cards are the no-winner + spend-share evidence below.
6
Vet the new team — don't accept a handoff on faith
"A reshuffle mid-engagement means I'm being handed a team I've never met or vetted. They may be excellent — but handing my account and ad budget to people I don't know, without seeing their track record, isn't something I can do responsibly on trust. That's not me being difficult — it's basic due diligence."
Ask — make him justify the team, don't accept "good hands":
  • Why the reshuffle? "What's actually driving the change from Ron & Sabrina?" — left, restructure, or performance? The answer tells you a lot.
  • Track record? "Walk me through James & Diane — what brands, what results, how long at this level?" Specifics, not adjectives.
  • Dedicated or shared? "How many brands are they each running? How much of their week is actually mine?" (Josh said Diane's across "a selection of our brands" — shared = divided attention.)
  • What does James do now? "I'm running creative in-house — so what does a Senior Creative Strategist own on my account, and where's the value vs what I already do?"
Leverage: "I'm not locking in the forward arrangement for a team I haven't met. Let's settle June fairly — I'll decide the go-forward once I've met James & Diane and seen what they bring." Keeps your optionality, and quietly questions paying senior rates for unvetted, shared resource.

💪 Your value argument — DELIVERY, not blame

The frame: Don't say "you caused my decline" — that's false (it started before them + the market) and rebuttable. Say: "You were hired to produce winning creative, and in 12 weeks you didn't." That's about the deal, not the market — and it's un-rebuttable.

Evidence 1 — no winner. Their best ad spent under $6k across the entire engagement. A real winner scales — my Black Friday creative did $26k in a single month. Theirs never broke out.

Evidence 2 — the algorithm voted. Of my top 20 ads by spend (last 3 months), only 4 are theirs. Meta scales whatever performs — it had every chance to back their creative and kept choosing mine.

If CPA comes up: use it as your reason to right-size — "the business is at its tightest, CPA's at an all-time high, that's why I have to cut every cost" — never as "you did this to me." Own the decline as market + my own pipeline; hold them only on "didn't deliver the upside I paid for."

Guardrails so you can't be poked: Don't attack their ROAS — their few scaled ads ran ~2.2–2.75, around your account average. Don't say "they made nothing" — they made edits; say "nothing that won." Confirm it's 4 of 20, not 3, before you say a number — or just say "a handful."

His rebuttals → your counters

"We still made ads for you in June — we did the work."
"Of course, and I'm not asking anyone to work for nothing. But let's be real about what June was — lighter-touch editing and recutting of my existing assets, not new production, no shoots, no creators, and not the senior team (Ron, Sabrina) I originally signed up for. A right-sized fee just matches the right-sized scope. I'm asking it to be proportionate to what the month actually involved, not to wipe it." (Don't overclaim "you made nothing" — they did ship the EOFYS promo edits. Stay on "lighter-touch / mostly my assets.")
"Our costs are fixed / can't flex."
"I get that, and I'm not asking you to eat a loss — just some give on a transition month where the creative's already winding down on your side. Plus the Rizza cost, which is its own thing."
"We took a loss for you last month (May)."
"Appreciate it — but let's be precise. Old deal vs new, May cost you about $5k less — ~$16k down to ~$11k inc GST. And that drop only happened because the performance cap triggered — May spend came in under the $100k target. So that's a performance clause doing its job, not a favour. The fee dropped because the results did." (Don't say "my business lost money because of your ads" — rebuttable. Stay on: the cap is a performance penalty (~$5k), and Rizza ~$2,150 is your real out-of-pocket loss.)
"Notice period / can't be retroactive."
"I'm honouring the agreement and the notice — just asking whether June can start the new structure given we're already mid-transition. As a goodwill thing, not a demand."
"Rizza was free / no extra charge from us."
"Free on your side or not, the double-booking created a real cost for me — I owe creators ~$2,150 for content I can't use. That's the bit I'm asking us to square."

Landing zones

👍 Acceptable Meaningful June reduction + Rizza made whole.
🟦 Floor Rizza made whole + some June goodwill.

Don'ts

  • Don't plead "I can't afford you" — own the decision, don't beg.
  • Don't blame them for your CPA / decline — it predates them & it's rebuttable. Hold them on "didn't deliver winners," not "you tanked it."
  • Don't attack their ROAS or say "they made nothing" — both are checkable and wrong. Stay on "no winner / nothing scaled."
  • Skip Google unless it's truly nothing-to-lose — you've never discussed it with him.
  • Don't relitigate the nitty-gritty timeline. Paint it simple: 12 weeks, no winner.

One breath: own June, ask for goodwill, make Rizza whole, stay off performance. You're negotiating from strength — you've already decided to right-size.
